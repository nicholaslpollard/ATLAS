# ATLAS living operating requirements — 2026-10-29

This document is the **current operating contract** for ATLAS historical options research, simulation and source acquisition. It consolidates the decisions and clarifications made during ATLAS review chats, including the Oct 29 V37–V42 work. Update this document whenever a user requirement changes, and keep `README.md`, `docs/roadmap.md` and individual research runbooks consistent with it.

## Mission, priorities and economics

1. Build a research platform that identifies **profitable, actionable trading strategies** across historical out-of-sample periods, ultimately supporting realistic combined-strategy portfolio simulation. Profitability matters more than repeatedly polishing inconsequential source diagnostics.
2. **Options are primary; stocks are the fallback** when options are not attractive or feasible. Strategy and universe changes may introduce new eligible tickers, contracts, strikes, expirations, dates, and exit observations.
3. Use causal prior-only inputs. The general trading decision clock is **09:35 ET**; a separately frozen SPY weekly research pilot has an original **10:00 ET** decision and **10:01 ET** order-eligible clock. Preserve each clock's own specification; never silently substitute the other.
4. Train/fine-tune a strategy only on earlier training folds. Test it on held-out future windows; protect explicitly reserved outcomes, including protected 2026 data when the frozen pilot specifies it. No look-ahead in feature selection, trade identity, parameter tuning, corporate actions, or market publication times.
5. Evaluate strategies **individually first**, tuning parameters and dynamic exits, then evaluate the selected strategies together under shared-cash, correlated-risk, capacity and simultaneous-order constraints. Measure gross and net P&L, costs, slippage, drawdown, concentration, exposure, fill/quote availability and abstentions.
6. Evaluate profitability and robustness by **weekly, monthly and annual windows** before combinations or promotion. Do not rely on a single pooled average to disguise persistently losing periods. No profitability claim without suitable source coverage, fees, realistic executions, and out-of-sample support.

## Historical options source coverage and storage

1. The long-term target is about **10 years** of appropriate full-universe contract history, including **hourly options data**, while also obtaining the exact-time quote, historical OI, Greeks and stock/underlying data needed by particular strategy revisions and dynamic exits.
2. Work within finite physical storage (approximately **228 GiB free** at the latest user report), using read-through provider APIs, SQLite/manifest indexes, lossless compression, immutable source receipts and sensible retention. Do not assume a multi-terabyte local database is available or affordable. Store what is acquired so it can be reused in later strategy runs.
3. **Permanent dynamic acquisition rule:** A changed strategy may discover a completely different set of ideal trades. Generate new **exact source requirements at runtime** from the current strategy parameters, underlying, expiration, strike, right, observation clock, and dynamically selected exit, with no frozen pilot/candidate roster restricting them.
4. Always **check the reusable source index first**. On a miss, automatically issue the necessary allowed read-only provider request, verify the response, losslessly archive the source, index its original query parameters, provider evidence and SHA-256, then make it available to qualifying research. New sources should be retained independently of whether that trade later wins or loses.
5. **Do not predeclare zero provider calls for future source-dependent runs**, because the actual demand is not known before evaluation. Do not insert artificial per-run or lifetime caps on the number of requested sources or candidates, and do not require manual typed authorization for every ThetaData GET. Provider entitlement and rate limits, finite physical headroom, safe per-response bounds, legal access, and source-integrity controls still apply; handle these with scheduling/chunking, caching, retries, or visible unresolved statuses rather than silently excluding trades.
6. Frozen cohorts/plans remain valuable as **reproducible historical tests**, but freezing a study never freezes the ability of other strategies or revisions to acquire new sources. A data request is associated with the strategy version and candidate while the underlying exact source receipt can be reused across strategy families.
7. Never convert an empty/failed provider response into implicit no-data or a zero exit value. Persist explicit no-data, errors, uncertain dispatch and source-evidence disagreements distinctly. Use retry/resume semantics appropriate to idempotent read-only requests; never fabricate a successful quote or mark. The V38/V39 architecture is a starting point, not a guarantee all legacy source archives or all simulators are connected.

## Runtime observability, efficiency and recovery — V42 requirement

1. **Every long acquisition or simulator run must show timestamped live console progress** in the local timezone, so the user can distinguish active work, a slow provider request and a crashed process. The run may make provider calls whenever source demand requires them.
2. Print an immediate start banner, milestone updates **every 10% of completed source requirements**, based on the known total (e.g., each 1 of 10, each 10 of 100, each 100 of 1,000). Do not print time-based heartbeats. Include last input/date/purpose, completed/total (when known), percentage, cached hits, successful newly archived/no-data sources, actual provider dispatch attempts, failures, elapsed time, throughput and an **observed, explicitly rough ETA**. No fake constant estimates or claims of fixed provider-call counts.
3. No separate progress log is required. Preserve successful source receipts and strategy-to-source lineage immediately, independent of final report creation. Report the final output path and the durable source-cache location.
4. A run with 1,083 source **requirements** does not necessarily make 1,083 provider calls. Cache hits may be immediate; slow provider/SDK authentication, large OI surfaces, throttling and disk failures can dominate runtime. ETA is not reliable until measured; it must adapt to throughput.
5. Avoid overwhelming the console with one line for every row or file. Use percentage-based milestones, including an explicit error when a source fails, while leaving the actual source acquisition unbounded by an arbitrary total-call quota.
6. Efficiency improvements should reduce **unnecessary provider requests** (exact caching, batching where correct, shared authentication, compressed retained archives), use concurrency only when source integrity and authenticated provider semantics are safe, and minimize repeated user-run cycles during the active **ThetaData Options Standard (~$80/mo)** subscription.

## Historical simulator source and account evidence gates

1. Original quote receipts, retrospectively computed features, predicted prices, hypothetical stress assumptions and broker executions are **different evidence classes**. Never label a quote-based simulated account as a genuine executed trade or a validated profitable strategy.
2. For the frozen SPY pilot, preserve original strike/expiry selection before reviewing later exit outcomes; the source tape and V37 report are retrospective research evidence, not confirmation of historical execution.
3. SPY prior completed 20 economic log returns, corporate-action publication times, 09:59 underlying source availability, historical rates and dividend inputs, prior-session open interest *publication* and exact 10:01 bid/ask/size/skew must each pass a point-in-time gate. An OI report published on decision morning may represent previous-session state; only actual time-qualified source evidence establishes its availability at the decision.
4. The frozen SPY pilot specifies an American-style **101-step Leisen–Reimer** implied-volatility computation for both legs (with independently qualified historical rate/dividend assumptions), and a comparison between strictly prior 20-return forecast calendar variance and same-maturity implied pair variance. The model cannot fill missing historical quotes, rate, dividends or OCC contract adjustments with invented defaults.
5. A market-quote midpoint is not a broker fill. Enter/exit eligibility, spreads, bid/ask sizes, quotes older than permitted freshness, call-put skew, early-exercise/dividend treatment, contract deliverables, commissions, slippage and orders must be accounted for explicitly. Missing exit evidence is **censored/unresolved**, not automatically zero terminal value except in clearly labeled stress scenarios.
6. As a strategy discovers qualified new candidates or changes stop/target/dynamic review times, **emit further source needs through V39/V38 on demand**, retain verified sources for later simulations, and preserve chronological cash/account state and outcome coverage.
